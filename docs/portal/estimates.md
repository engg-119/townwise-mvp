# Estimates: build contract

People in town, the town-local "today" TownwiseAI reads, the town calendar and people in town tools, "How Townwise estimates" (the model card), and (U13B) forecast runs: the store, staff import and publish, the published forecast read, `get_forecast` and the model card's run state. Spec 10.13 (routes and shapes), 13.2 (people in town) and 14.4 (forecast runs).

## 1. What exists, and what it honestly supports

- **Forecasts are published by the lab and by staff import.** No model runs in the API: a calibration job (the forecast lab, D27, and the weekly refresh, D42, section 8) or staff (an imported run) produce a run's outputs, and the system publishes a lab run when it passes the gate. Until a run is published for a location, the model card says so with an **In progress** tag, and `get_forecast` says "Townwise has no forecast for this location yet." Owner decision (2026-11-05): Sawtooth's frozen v3 forecast may be imported and published once Sawtooth is an organization in Townwise and the owner confirms its consent (consent given 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-15).
- **Hanover's calendar** is seeded from the committed public `modeling/dartmouth_calendar_2025_2026.csv` (13.1) and extended from the registrar's 2026-27 and 2027-28 key dates and Dartmouth's five-year calendar, so its term state is known from winter 2025 to spring 2028 finals. Other towns have key dates and holidays only.
- **No headcount input** (residents, students in college housing, enrollment, whether housing closes between terms, event attendance, visitors) is researched for any town (owner decision, 2026-11-05). `api/src/people-in-town/sources.ts` is committed empty; every headcount is null with its reason, and every surface says Townwise can't put a number on it yet.

Rules: a headcount only when every input has a stored source; every estimate a range; no free text from a model or a partner file; labels come from code and the public calendar.

## 5. Privacy

What reaches Anthropic through the two tools: public town data and source URLs (calendar, jobs index, researched counts), term states, and the location's own mean guests or net sales by term state and weekday with day counts. Never an email, a person's or upload's id, a note's text or author, or a file name (`api/test/chat-estimate-tools.test.ts` sentinels). For the privacy page (applied by its owner): the Anthropic item gains "public facts about your town (its calendar and who is likely in town)".

U13B, `get_forecast`: the location's published forecast ranges, code-made reasons with public source URLs, the model's version and dates, its accuracy, and the stored guests for past forecast days. Never the model, its features, training rows, the run file, the notebook commit, a stored calendar ref or a staff id (`api/test/chat-estimate-tools.test.ts`). Privacy page wording (applied in the forecast lab, D27; redrafted for autonomous forecasts, D42; approved by the owner on 2026-11-19; release B's year-ahead clause is spec 22.2 item 102): "Forecasts. Forecasts are on for every organization; if you don't want them, ask Townwise staff to turn yours off. Once a location's sales history spans at least 230 days, Townwise forecasts its daily guests up to a year ahead, the first three months as a forecast and the rest as a wider outlook, and keeps that forecast current by itself: each week, if anything has changed since the last fit, a background job on Townwise's own servers refits it from that location's own daily guest counts, the closures, private events and bookings in its notes and business rules, and the public town calendar. The job sees no other location's days. Each forecast is made by Townwise's trained demand model or, when the location's history is too short for that model or a simpler baseline did better on its past weeks, by that baseline, built from its recent weeks and the town calendar; the app says which made it. A new forecast goes live only when it passes Townwise's accuracy checks. What a forecast learns from your restaurant is used only for your restaurant: it is not used for other restaurants, and it never trains AI models such as TownwiseAI's. Townwise keeps the results (a range of guests per day, the dates the forecast learned from, the settings it was made with, and how accurate it has been) and not the fitted model. When Townwise staff import a forecast instead, it was built on the team's own computers from sales exports you sent us; that model and those exports stay with Townwise staff, are used only for your restaurant, and are deleted when your organization is deleted or when you ask. ## 8. Forecast runs (U13B)

Rules: spec 14.4. Staff export with `modeling/export_forecast_run.py` (standard library only; reads the frozen CSVs in `modeling/forecasts/` after checking their sha256 against the values given, maps the guest columns, writes only to the gitignored `modeling/outputs/`, prints counts and never a figure), import the file from P21 as a draft, check it, and publish it. The run file is outputs only; the API never computes a forecast and never holds a model (lab runs, below, are computed by a job, not the API). Run files trained on days owners uploaded to Townwise (`trainingSource: 'townwise_uploads'`) are refused until the team decides whether staff may use uploaded days for research (spec 14.1, DECISION NEEDED).

Forecast lab (spec D27, 14.4, 10.15): since 2026-11-09 a run can also come from a calibration. A job on Townwise's Azure servers (`modeling/lab`) refits a location's model on that location's own uploaded days and notes, with the notebook's method, and posts a draft run (`trainingSource: 'townwise_lab'`) and a scorecard to the API, which keeps outputs only; the fitted model is stored nowhere, and its code version, parameters and input hash are recorded on the run (`lab` block) so it can be reproduced. The system publishes a draft only while the organization's forecasts are on (on by default since 2026-11-16; staff can switch one off) and it passes the gate in `api/src/lab/gate.ts` (at least 12 held-out weeks, at least half a point lower weekly WAPE than the published model on the same weeks, 80% ranges holding 65% to 95% of weeks, weekday bias no more than 2 points worse; a first forecast needs 12 backtest weeks with an 80% range, which 595 days of history always gives, and a weekday-median baseline beaten; a calibration needed those 595 days to start until 2026-11-19, when a person’s start took the weekly refresh’s 230 days, the baseline alone running under 595, spec D42), and staff can withdraw it. "The API never computes a forecast and never holds a model" stays true; the job computes and keeps no model. The reader, the live accuracy and the model card treat a lab run like any published run (the card says "trained on sales exports you sent Townwise" for an imported run and "trained on sales history and notes you uploaded to Townwise" for a lab run, `trainedOnWords` in `client/src/portal/estimates/api.ts`). Run files with `trainingSource: 'townwise_uploads'` stay refused through the staff import until the research-copy decision (spec 14.1). Autonomous forecasts (spec D42, 14.6, OpenSpec change `autonomous-forecasts`) changed this in phases, all built and on by default (reads of up to 92 days, `accuracy`, `scored`, `engine` and the baseline's label "Townwise's baseline from your recent weeks and the town calendar", and the family `seasonal_baseline`, then the rest below). As built: the API starts a calibration of every changed location of a switched-on organization each Monday from 03:00 in the town, paid by Townwise and outside the 4 a day, from a 230-day span; the lab runs a baseline engine (each weekday's recent level times last year's seasonal shape times the location's own calendar effects) beside the model, which still needs 595 days, and returns the current engine's run unless the other is at least 0.5 points better on the same backtest weeks; and every draft, scheduled or started by a person, is judged by one set of rules: a same-engine refit within 0.5 points of the best recorded backtest in its streak (a tie publishes), each engine's absolute checks (a baseline must beat the weekday median), and a draft that moves the next 28 days by more than 25% held for staff. Runs then reach today plus 91 days, capped at 182 days after the last day of sales, while the gate keeps judging 28 days ahead. "The API never computes a forecast and never holds a model" stays true: both engines run in the lab job.

### Notes on read

Rules: spec 14.5 (D30). From the town's today on, the forecast reader (`api/src/forecasts/reader.ts`) adjusts each published day with the location's notes on every read (`api/src/forecasts/adjust.ts`, pure): a private event with more guests and a guest count adds its guests to `low`, `mid` and `high`, a buyout (a private event, closed) books the day, a closure closes it, and every other note is listed with why it changed nothing; a closure note beats every other note, and on a day the model closed a buyout books it and a private event with a guest count opens it with only those guests. Runs and their rows are never rewritten, so frozen and published runs stay as made, and an edited or deleted note shows on the next read: the forecast route, `get_forecast` and `show_visual` (whose band is the day's range with the notes, as `get_forecast` gives it; the 80% range is the model's own, `day.model`). Past days and `inRange` stay the model's own, and live accuracy leaves out closed and likely-closed days. A business rule's schedule (D31) applies as a note does, but a note on the day that decides it beats the rule's closure or buyout. A day carries `model` (the run's figures), `noteGuests`, `moreNotes` and `moreRules` (at most 6 note and 6 rule reasons a day); a note's reason has `kind: 'note'`, a label made in code from the note's kind, guest count and hours (never its detail) and `note: { id, guests, outcome }`. A week's range shifts by each day's adjusted middle less the model's (a newly closed or booked day takes its model middle off, added guests add, never below 0), and is left out only when a changed day has no model middle. The lab's input (`api/src/lab/input.ts`) leaves the notes this read applies after `dataThrough` to it (`closed` and `booked` false, `privateEvent` false for a buyout or a counted private event), so a lab run never counts the party twice and never bakes in a closure or buyout that editing or deleting the note can't undo; runs made before that keep their baked statuses until the next calibration. On history days a counted party's guest count comes off the day's guests and sets no `privateEvent`, so training sees base demand and the read adds the party back. Tests: `api/test/forecast-adjust.test.ts`, `forecast-reasons.test.ts`, `estimates.int.test.ts`, `lab-input.test.ts`, `chat-estimate-tools.test.ts`, `chat-visual.test.ts`.
